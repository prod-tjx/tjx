Problem Statement · FY26Q3

Problem Statement

The Trader team had no automated path to decisioning while adjacent programs scaled through automation, TFA review remained fully manual and capacity-bound.
🧑‍💻
Manual Analyst Review
Every trader-flagged order required individual analyst review. Throughput was capped directly by human capacity — there was no way to scale without adding headcount.
Capacity Constraint
📈
Unpredictable Volume Spikes
Automated actioning from other programs pushed large volumes of orders to D68 trader blocks simultaneously. The trader team absorbed these spikes with no tooling to match the pace.
Cross-Program Impact
⏱️
Always Reacting, Never Ahead
Orders near commit date were actioned in large batches by adjacent programs. Without an automated response, the trader team was perpetually catching up — never operating at the same speed.
Decisioning Gap
Cross-Program Volume Challenge
Adjacent Program Automation Was Driving the Backlog
Programs like DGF and FA2 had established automated actioning pipelines. When their systems fired at scale, orders near commit date landed on D68 trader blocks in large batches — many of them true positives close to their commit window. The trader team had no equivalent automation, meaning every downstream consequence of another program's decisioning required a manual analyst response. The gap between automated upstream decisioning and fully manual trader review created a structural capacity problem.
